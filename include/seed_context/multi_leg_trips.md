# AstroTrips Multi-Leg Trips and Layover Policy

## Overview

AstroTrips is built around flexible travel. If you want to stay longer at a
destination, break your journey into segments, or visit more than one place on
a single trip, we can almost always make it work. We call this a multi-leg
trip, and the extra time you spend at a stop along the way is a layover.

This document explains how multi-leg trips work, when a layover needs a
shuttle, and how the cost is driven mostly by one thing: whether you hold a
shuttle license. Read the shuttle license section carefully, because it is the
single biggest factor in what a layover will cost you.

## What is a multi-leg trip

A standard trip flies you from origin to destination and back on a scheduled
vessel. A multi-leg trip splices that journey so you can:

- Stay longer at your destination than the standard return schedule allows.
- Stop at an intermediate location and spend time there before continuing.
- Combine several destinations into one itinerary.

Each segment between stops is a leg. The time you spend at a stop is a layover.

## Direct versus connecting routes

Whether a layover is simple or needs extra logistics depends on the route.

- On a direct route, the vessel travels straight to your destination and back
  on a published schedule. To extend your stay, you usually just rejoin a later
  scheduled vessel. No shuttle is involved.
- On a connecting route, the journey passes through one or more intermediate
  points. If you want to break your trip at one of those points, or your dates
  no longer line up with a scheduled vessel, you may need a shuttle to reach or
  leave the layover point.

## When you need a shuttle

A shuttle bridges the gap when no scheduled vessel serves your layover point on
the dates you want.

- Direct routes rarely need a shuttle. Extending a stay is usually a matter of
  rebooking onto a later vessel.
- Connecting and outer-planet routes are more likely to need a shuttle,
  especially Europa and Titan, where scheduled vessels are less frequent.

When a shuttle is required, a shuttle cost applies on top of your trip fare.

## Shuttle cost depends on your shuttle license

This is the key cost driver, so we explain both cases in full.

### If you have a shuttle license

- You pilot the shuttle yourself.
- You pay only to rent the shuttle.
- This is the cheaper option by a wide margin, because there is no pilot to pay.

### If you do not have a shuttle license

- You pay to rent the shuttle and you also pay for a pilot to fly it.
- The pilot must stay on the destination planet for the duration of your
  layover, because there is no scheduled vessel to take them back.
- You pay for the pilot's time for the whole layover. On a seven-day layover,
  that means seven pilot-days on top of the shuttle rental.
- Be aware that renting a pilot for a full week or longer can get expensive.
  For longer outer-planet layovers, the pilot cost can rival the trip fare
  itself.

## Reducing the pilot cost

If you do not have a license, there is one main way to lower the pilot cost:

- If you can arrange for another party to have the pilot pick them up, the
  pilot does not sit idle on the planet, and your share of the pilot cost drops
  accordingly.
- Arranging that hand-off is the customer's responsibility. It is your trip to
  coordinate.
- AstroTrips will help when we already have an AstroTrips-sponsored pilot
  routed through that planet. In that case we can often share the pilot and
  reduce your cost. If we do not have a sponsored pilot on that route, you are
  free to organize the hand-off yourself.

## Cost drivers at a glance

- Shuttle rental rate, charged per day.
- Pilot day rate, which applies only if you do not hold a shuttle license.
- Layover duration. Longer layovers mean more shuttle-days and, without a
  license, more pilot-days.
- Route and destination. Outer-planet routes cost more to service.

## Illustrative example

A traveller wants a seven-day layover on Titan.

- With a shuttle license: shuttle rental for the shuttle only.
- Without a shuttle license: shuttle rental plus seven pilot-days, because the
  pilot stays on Titan for the full week unless a pickup is arranged.

The license holder pays a fraction of what the unlicensed traveller pays for
the same layover.

## Per-destination notes

- Moon and Venus: usually direct, layovers are simple, and a shuttle is rarely
  needed.
- Mars: direct or connecting depending on the launch window. A shuttle is
  sometimes needed for off-schedule layovers.
- Europa and Titan: outer-planet and typically connecting. Shuttle and pilot
  logistics are most common here, and the pilot cost matters most.

## Consider a shuttle license

Because a license removes the pilot cost entirely, it pays for itself quickly
for anyone who plans more than the occasional multi-leg trip. AstroTrips can
point you toward a shuttle certification course and let you know which routes
your license will cover.

## We love customized trips

Multi-leg travel is where AstroTrips shines. We try to make all sorts of
customized itineraries possible, including unusual stops and long layovers. The
only thing we ask is that you go in with eyes open about the shuttle and pilot
costs when you do not hold a license.

## Booking a multi-leg trip

Contact the AstroTrips travel desk with the stops you want and the dates you
have in mind. We will map the legs, tell you where a shuttle is needed, confirm
whether an AstroTrips-sponsored pilot is available, and give you a full quote
before you commit.
